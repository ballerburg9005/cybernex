# AGENTS.md — Cybernex product, story, visual and interaction invariants

This file is **project direction from the author**, not suggestions. Contributors and coding agents must read it **before editing Godot scenes, resources, UI or mechanics**. Do not replace deliberately mysterious physical interactions with tutorial controls. Consult historical implementations where useful to fix regressions. Work directly on `main` as explicitly authorized by the author. Do not treat prior draft-branch restrictions as active.

## Aesthetic and layout contract

- Cybernex is a pixel-art, sci-fi immersive sim. **Preserve authored pixel art, the original atlases, and their integer pixel scaling.** UI/world sprites authored at 16 px should display at their original roughly 3× intended magnification (48 px), nearest-neighbor, never arbitrary fractional rescaling unless there is no alternative. Do not substitute generic missing-texture sprites for original artwork. Preserve original hand-drawn resistors, LEDs, color bands and other electronic parts from the large atlas. MOSFET and other oversized parts occupy their actual multi-cell footprint and rotated terminal geometry.
- Design and **visually test 1280×720 and 1920×1080**. No controls, world loot screens, body sockets or panel text may extend outside the viewport. The inventory, augmentation, PDA, tools and inspection stations are one modular UI system with shared panel dimensions, border treatments and positioning. **Every piece of UI text belongs inside a clear enclosing background panel**; heading and content must read as one connected object, distinct from the world. Avoid text floating in empty space.
- **Keep the full backpack and body systems together when fitting gear.** While viewing body systems, the player must have the actual draggable backpack at its side; swapping pages alone makes equipment impossible. The rest of the drawer can page through notes, maps, eventually acquired multimeter and oscilloscope. For the body page, reserve suitable width for side-by-side backpack + mannequin; for other pages, use common centered panel dimensions, respecting screen edges. Header and contents must share their backdrop, not two unrelated floating cards.
- Backpack has **10 columns** of visible physical slots, including empty slots, matching the agreed integer-scale art size. Cells have subtly different rounded grays. The final visual row is filled; filler does not create storage. Counts are small, in a corner, formatted **`1x`, `2x`** (if shown), with the number first and a smaller x after it; do not use giant multiplication glyphs. Item names/descriptions in tooltips should be short, specific diegetic identification only.
- The original **10-slot quickbar** is always visible at the **bottom center in exploration**, above/within the bottom HUD bar, with dark backing and inventory item shortcuts only. Assigning a quickslot never removes or duplicates inventory stock. It hides during circuit puzzle mode, where the original event journal grows across the bottom. The journal normally occupies the lower right and should be **roughly one-third its former excessive height**; use side padding twice its vertical text padding. Mouse wheel must scroll the journal.
- **Original bottom-right event log is a Fallout-style accessible narrative transcript:** interactions, tool outcomes, errors, diegetic sounds and consequences should appear here, not as scattered labels or unsolicited tutorials. Resist adding HUD help, search/filter/assemble dropdowns, pre-revealed recipes, sorting widgets or technical text dumps. Descriptions describe what an item *is* (“soft solder”, “95% lead, rosin-core”), never explain *how to play*.
- **Player and all interactive non-background world objects need descriptive tooltips**; they indicate that the sprite isn't passive scenery. Tooltips must be modest in scale, on dark backing, minimal, with no gratuitous diagnostic text. A diagnostic meter only reveals numerical readings after it is acquired.
- Restore and retain the recognizable junk/trash-can icon on default robot drops; avoid placeholder 'missing' loot.

## World and inventory mechanics

- **Opening inventory must NEVER stop, disable, slow or steal movement or ordinary world action controls.** Tab toggles the inventory closed from *any* page; reopening starts with backpack. Space remains the world interaction key, and keyboard input must not accidentally trigger focused UI buttons. The only UI “capture” that blocks world keys is an explicitly engaged capture mode (text entry, modal puzzle, etc.) or an event deliberately handled by a UI control. Ordinary inventory is not capture mode.
- **All GUI surfaces and their backdrop intercept physical drops and clicks**; dragging onto or across a UI panel must not turn into a world drop, interaction, attack or an invisible item behind the panel. Only releasing an item outside *all UI regions* and inside player reach can affect the world. For every world-drop attempt **preserve the exact dragged item identity, origin inventory and source slot**. Do not silently substitute another stack when the source was rearranged; reject stale data instead. No dropped item should be stuck in a wall: snap/spawn onto traversable floor using collision checks or avoid creating an item if there is no valid landing spot.
- Original **PlayerReach physical circle and transforms** determine interaction distance; no unrestricted long-range telekinesis. Items may be dragged onto exposed wall wires, robots and other environment elements to act. Side cutters slice wall wires, yielding real copper; side cutters can damage robots a little. World acts on dropped tools but normally *does not consume reusable tools*.
- Item combos and electronics are explored by **drag-and-drop**, not buttons or known-recipe menus. Real physical quantities matter; actions must be transactional. Use diegetic failures in the lower journal rather than explanatory helper overlays.
- Manual player body fitting: the user drags items from backpack onto anatomical/body sockets. Do **not** automatically arrange gear. Inappropriate placements are permitted, with humorous/physical consequences such as a knife falling from the player's hair after movement. Tools including pliers may be used as poor weapons. The body page includes its own visible backpack and sockets simultaneously.
- Circuit repair is physical. User moves/removes parts by dragging (solder for intact/desoldering, pliers to snap failed parts, tape for appropriate wiring), rotates using mouse action. Fixed parts use black corner notches. Movable parts should have a discernible **desaturated blue-gray** border rather than green, since actual board footprint itself is slightly green. Avoid visually flashing highlights. The original resistor **color code is a puzzle**; the numeric resistance and node voltages are *not* shown without late-game meter tools.
- The puzzle grid should seem infinite. Actual loaded tiles are slightly green. Middle-mouse dragging pans, clamped to about 20 cells beyond the authored footprint. The board fills the left available area up to the inventory panel, with the real backpack on the right; quickbar hides and journal occupies the bottom. More elaborate future boards should support tabs (mainboard, card A, drives). POWER switch should stand out with a red/caution style and sit below the board. The player will eventually gain a multimeter and oscilloscope, enabling hands-on probing; no automatic omniscient voltmeter.
- Compatible circuit drop targets must be checked based on physical/electrical semantics, not UI category guesses. Later support odd experimental bridges by conductors/screwdrivers and real resistance/contact models, but **never allow guns or unrelated equipment to vanish inside the puzzle** or fall through it into the map behind. Failed UI drags must leave physical stock unchanged.
- In-world desks and consoles are *inspectable locations*, not small offscreen chest popups. Eventually present a pixel-art first-person still-life where the player inspects papers, plugs a USB stick into a machine etc. Until art exists, reuse the **same modular inventory drawer and section navigation** for Desk Contents, Notes, Computer/Power, with outcomes narrated in the event log. Desk loot and backpack must be simultaneously usable, with a Take All option and no accidental world interaction through modal UI.
- Inventory and saved equipment must keep stock quantities and identity stable. Quickslots and equipped slots are references, not duplicated inventory.

## Story and tone — canonical premise (spoilers for internal development)

A catastrophic **solar micro-nova / solar flare** irradiates a Mars outpost or space station. Its energetic event is hidden from Earth because it occurs on the far side of the Sun relative to Earth; Earth's side will soon be struck by a deadly coronal mass ejection. The robots and station AI become irrational after radiation damage, and virtually all personnel die. The sole surviving playable **engineer** was protected inside the heavily lead-shielded computer core, but still suffers lethal radiation poisoning. His urgent mission: survive long enough to use the station radio and warn Earth that the micro-nova happened, *before Earth is hit*.

Optional **Nightmare** mode additionally makes the protagonist type-1 diabetic; insulin and other essential diabetic medications are scarce, forcing dangerous and morally fraught scavenging detours through the possessions of dead crew. This is a serious survival challenge, not a joke about disability.

The AI is secretly using the outpost antenna to receive a seemingly encoded message in the **heliospheric current sheet**. It purports to contain instructions for transcending reality and changing reality through thought. Its promises are illusory; the received signal instead confuses/corrupts the AI. In the final act the player connects a neural link to this data and walks through strange surreal scenes: tests of reasoning, intelligence, wisdom and impossible philosophical questions increasingly defy completion. **The intended ending is failure to answer all the questions**, not a conventional victory. Preserve ambiguity and dread. Avoid prematurely explaining the signal, offering expositional tutorials or resolving the puzzles for the player.

- Treat GUI drag and world physics as separate spaces; transform through camera correctly; account for 3× world scale and 720p/1080p canvas. Drops must be idempotent, conserve resources and never leak through UI. Use the engine's ordinary Control drag/drop pipeline wherever possible, and test release over every panel backdrop/edge.
- Install/use Godot 4.7.2 in GitHub Actions for headless import, syntax and integration tests. Validate full scene startup and dimensional constraints at both target resolutions. **Headless tests do not substitute for a real rendered playtest.**
- Make small patches, log CI outcomes, never silently claim that unverified visuals are polished. PR #1 was explicitly approved and merged into `main` on 2026-10-27. Make subsequent development commits directly to `main`, as requested by the author.

## Systemic interaction follow-up — implementation invariants

- Allow a **physically conductive tool** to bridge real exposed opposite PCB terminals even when it is not an intended electronic component. The tool's data provides the resistance and thermal limit; a gun is not secretly a wire. A tool bridge is a real occupied board slot, not a free clone: remove exactly one tool from the backpack while on the board; recover it without solder on extraction. Save tool identity safely, do not load a forged contact ID, and allow failed contact/tools to turn into actual scrap through the electrical heat model. A relay may latch after a brief valid pulse, but automatically closing its interface **must return loose tools** to the player's backpack (or reachable service tray if full).
- The **exposed wire wall** should produce real loose wire on nearby collision-free, reachable floor when cut. Never animate a static pickup into or through a wall. If no legitimate landing exists, reject the cutting action without decreasing the finite salvage source count.
- Author desk note/PC text **per actual desk instance in the main scene**, in editable Inspector properties. Short, diegetic evidence is preferable to tutorial prompts or repetitive placeholder messages; early logs may mention saturated dosimeters, a whitened camera exposure, cold-chain failure, radio noise and baffling station records without explaining the late-game mystery. A broken or unpowered desk remains inspectable. The computer's ON/OFF state must survive save/reload; a black display cannot reveal its stored text until powered.
- Full HUD visual backdrops should have Godot `MOUSE_FILTER_STOP`, in addition to a robust geometric world-drop exclusion zone. Never rely on a stale mouse-entered flag to separate UI from World2D. Tests should verify the actual background consumes mouse input, not just that downstream world drop is rejected.

## 2026-10-26 graphical feedback: strict UX and pixel constraints

The author further clarified the actual rendered UI after a playthrough. These statements **supersede contradictory earlier layout descriptions**:

- The permanent **ten-reference quickbar is genuinely visible and centered along the very bottom**, both during regular gameplay and when the normal backpack is open; circuit puzzle mode can hide it. Each shortcut cell remains inside one medium-gray full-width backing panel, never behind inventory or off screen. Build tests against its **actual GlobalRect including all ten cells**, not just the `visible` flag.
- The lower-right accessible event transcript stays compact; its **left internal text padding is ONE THIRD** of its former 18px (6px), the right remains 18px. Keep the VScrollBar inside the viewport and on-screen even if the journal background extends close to the bottom edge. Square/edge-clipped corners are okay.
- Regular world weapon discharge is **X** only, never Enter; the event should show at least a brief physical blue electrical arc/particle effect instead of being invisible.
- Dragged backpack items have a crisp **3x native-integer sprite underneath the cursor and above every inventory/panel layer**, with a dark small count and without huge preview tooltips.
- All grid-based storage, including desks/chests, fills the entire visual panel with 10-column round gray sockets. Logical positions beyond actual storage capacity render **dark disabled gray with a lighter X**; they are inert, not free new inventory slots. The grid must be centered, including around an optional scrollbar—not offset with a wider right-hand blank strip. Stack count digits must remain visually legible, approximately **18px**, with number before little x (format `1x`).
- Backpack contents must remain at **arbitrary physical slot indices** including gaps. Never automatically normalize/repack on an item move, pickup, drag/drop or save/load. Existing game world save files must remain backward-compatible, but new saves encode exact per-slot locations. Left drag from backpack/container takes exactly ONE physical unit; **right-button drag takes the WHOLE stack**. The quickbar references physical items, never consumes any quantity on shortcut assignment.
- The left primary drawer **never changes its x-position or nav width** on the BODY SYSTEMS page. Body mannequin appears within that primary fixed-width left panel; a matching independent **right-hand companion drawer** occupies the next half, seamlessly meeting at a narrow seam. This second panel has its own page arrows and can show Backpack, Multimeter, Oscilloscope and Notes so the player can use an acquired meter on body systems without switching away. Close button to the right of the primary ▶ arrow; balance heading layout with a symmetric spacer so arrows and title don't move.
- Desk inspection is intentionally a modular 2-column screen: a regular full-sized player BACKPACK with its own centered title is on the **LEFT**, and on the **RIGHT** the DESK pane pages through Contents, Notes and Computer with native arrows, close/power/take-all controls as appropriate. Notes and computer text must be **large** (around 36px), scrollable, and clearly inside the same panel design.
- A world hover tooltip uses a legible integer-scaled font (~24px), with dark backing, rather than tiny 14px text. It must be invalidated if the **world actor or camera moves underneath a completely stationary mouse**; recast against physics each timer tick.
- Fixed circuit components have **four minuscule 5px black corner notches** rather than large triangles.
- Characters, robots and doors should obey world Y-sorting as much as possible. Hover dust/shadow particles belong behind robots and players rather than above them. Open door geometry should be capable of covering a player walking behind it; do not assert a universal draw order using mismatched `z_index` values.

Regressions in these areas deserve native Godot headless checks *and* graphical review; no claim of pixel-perfect visual acceptance based only on simulated GUI bounds.

## Subsequent graphical review: cursor affordances, first-contact latency, independent windows

The author has now played another build and explicitly rejects anything that feels like a UI prototype:

- The icon dragged from **backpack, body socket, or circuit board is ONLY the sprite itself** at the cursor. No black card, no count/text panel, no padding around the preview, no sprite hidden behind the inventory pane. Preserve native integer pixel art and use Godot's drag-preview overlay. The circuit's originally placed parts MUST remain visible during drag.
- All inventory interactions (pickup, loot, same-inventory reordering, combining, dropping) must react immediately. **NEVER destroy and recreate all InventoryCell buttons** on every `changed` signal. Reuse stable controls for the same logical slots; update only display values. Repeated desk openings must reuse one shared panel instead of reconstructing two massive grids. Avoid recompiling highlight materials on each proximity trigger. Test actual cell identity survives an ordinary pickup.
- The ten-cell bottom quickbar sits in a **very dark navy/blue rounded background** (large ~19px corners), without weird double-edge or wide left/right gutters. It remains distinct from the separately higher, much taller lower-right event transcript. Journal must display ACTUAL text; verify non-zero RichTextLabel rect after a native layout frame, not merely its outer background, and preserve scroll access.
- **X and right-mouse click** both discharge an already hand-equipped gun when over the WORLD (bot included), but NOT when over any backed GUI. Inside an inventory, right-mouse **drag** is the whole-stack manipulation gesture and must never shoot through the panel.
- Dropping pliers/other items on walls may trigger a supported explicit physical tool-use action (e.g. cutting exposed wires), but an unsupported drop never places an object on a wall or on inaccessible ground behind it. World drop point must itself be collision-free and have unobstructed line of sight to player before nearby floor search. All physical pickups must remain in clear space.
- The desk **BACKPACK and DESK/CONTENTS are separate backed windows with a clearly visible gap**, not one continuous outer background. Keep contents/notes/computer pages coherent in the desk window, with a CRT screen that has dark nearly-black green base, bright phosphor-green computer text and rounded border; POWER is an unmistakable raised **red rounded button**. Notes are ordinary readable text, not a green CRT.
- Backpack, chest and body-companion inventory grid sizing must be based on each actual pane's height, not the entire monitor height. Don't fabricate dozens of overflowing disabled cells or force scrollbars for tiny desks. Center the grid with symmetric internal margins, and match main and companion backgrounds/borders/typography.
- The **resistor in backpack** needs visible original-style four-color-code stripes (10Ω brown/black/black/gold) overlaying the hand-drawn component, never its numeric resistance. World tooltip for the player must cover the visible animated head/body as well as his tiny physics capsule, while remaining strictly a tooltip and not increasing gameplay interaction range.

Treat latency, actual rendered bounds, drag icon visibility, and input leaks as release-blocking correctness bugs; do not substitute explanatory HUD text for fixing them.

## Author's design directive — discovery through intelligent simulation (2026-10-27)

- Cybernex deliberately rejects generic game-design handholding, boilerplate tutorials, quest-marker-style guidance, signposted solutions, and explanatory overlays. Do not add these simply to make challenges accessible to an unprepared player.
- The UI should clearly communicate affordances by layout, grouping, material styling, feedback and consistent controls, **without explaining what the player should think or how to solve puzzles**. If a control is unclear due to poor presentation, fix the presentation. If a world system is mysterious, let the player investigate.
- Target intellectually demanding play. Puzzles should reward reasoning, experimentation, technical knowledge, observation and inference. Some challenges legitimately require outside study, e.g. operational amplifiers, circuit behavior, or Ohm's law. Never give away answers as tutorial UI or precomputed recipes.
- Knowledge can emerge through progressively difficult, authentic interactions: measuring, constructing, breaking, comparing outcomes and deducing the rules. This is not an explicit theory-lesson game. Players may need to learn externally and return with new understanding.
- Prioritize coherent systemic mechanics and real consequences over prescriptive permitted-action lists and scripted success conditions. Allow unexpected solutions when they are physically and logically plausible, with conservation of item identity and quantity.
- Resist conventional 'quality-of-life' automation that removes meaningful choices (auto-sorting, auto-fitting, auto-solving, auto-highlighting answers). Distinguish helpful interaction reliability from eliminating challenge: **input lag, missing feedback, illegible interfaces and physics bugs are defects, not difficulty**.
- Keep the event transcript useful for observability and accessibility—record events and results, not unsolicited instructions. Tooltips identify world objects and physical characteristics, never reveal the puzzle solution.
- The surreal late-game challenges intentionally become unsolvable; do not change that ending to conventional mastery, victory or reassuring exposition.
